Cut

A clean break in the skin with straight edges.

What to do now

  1. Press a clean cloth firmly on the cut for 10 minutes. Do not keep lifting it to check.
  2. Raise the injured part above heart level.
  3. Rinse with clean running water for 5 minutes. Remove dirt gently.
  4. Cover with a sterile bandage or gauze.

Care for the next days

  • Change the dressing daily, or when wet or dirty.
  • Keep it clean and dry for the first day, then wash gently with mild soap.
  • Apply a thin layer of antibiotic ointment if you wish.
  • Check that your tetanus shot is within 10 years.

See a doctor if

  • Bleeding does not stop after 10 minutes of pressure.
  • The cut is deep, gaping, or longer than 1 cm on the face.
  • You see redness spreading, pus, swelling, or have fever.
Buy at a medical shop: sterile gauze, adhesive bandages, antiseptic solution, antibiotic ointment.

Scrape

Skin rubbed off, often from a fall. Surface looks raw and may ooze.

What to do now

  1. Rinse well under clean running water to remove grit.
  2. Clean the skin around it with mild soap. Keep soap out of the wound.
  3. Pat dry with clean gauze.
  4. Cover with a non-stick dressing.

Care for the next days

  • Change the dressing daily and keep the area moist with ointment.
  • Do not pick the scab.
  • Protect healed skin from sun for a few weeks.

See a doctor if

  • Dirt or gravel stays in the wound after cleaning.
  • The area is large, or very painful.
  • You notice pus, warmth, or red streaks.
Buy at a medical shop: non-stick dressings, saline wash, antibiotic ointment, medical tape.

Puncture

A hole from a nail, needle, or sharp point. Often looks small but can be deep.

What to do now

  1. Let it bleed a little. Then stop bleeding with gentle pressure.
  2. Rinse with clean running water for 5 minutes.
  3. Do not remove a deeply embedded object. Stabilise it and go to hospital.
  4. Cover with a clean bandage.

Care for the next days

  • Change the dressing daily.
  • Watch closely for infection for several days.
  • Ask about a tetanus shot, especially for rusty or dirty objects.

See a doctor today

  • Almost all punctures benefit from a check for tetanus risk.
  • Object is stuck, wound is deep, or it is on the foot, chest, or belly.
  • Swelling, throbbing pain, pus, or fever.
Buy at a medical shop: gauze, antiseptic, bandages. A tetanus shot needs a clinic.

Burn

Skin hurt by heat, hot liquid, fire, or chemicals.

What to do now

  1. Cool under cool (not ice-cold) running water for 20 minutes.
  2. Remove rings and tight items before swelling starts. Do not pull off stuck clothing.
  3. Cover loosely with clean cling film or a non-stick dressing.
  4. Do not pop blisters. Do not use ice, butter, or toothpaste.

Care for the next days

  • Change the dressing daily and keep it clean.
  • Use paracetamol for pain if you can safely take it.
  • Keep healed skin out of the sun.

Go to hospital if

  • The burn is bigger than your palm, or on the face, hands, feet, joints, or genitals.
  • Skin is white, leathery, or charred.
  • It is a chemical or electrical burn, or the person is a child or elderly.
Buy at a medical shop: burn dressings, sterile non-stick pads, paracetamol.

Bruise or swelling

Skin intact but sore, discoloured, or swollen after a knock.

What to do now

  1. Rest the area.
  2. Apply a cold pack wrapped in cloth for 15 minutes, every few hours.
  3. Use a snug elastic bandage, not tight.
  4. Raise the area above heart level.

Care for the next days

  • After 2 days, a warm compress can ease stiffness.
  • Most bruises fade in 1 to 2 weeks.

See a doctor if

  • You cannot move or put weight on the area, or it looks bent.
  • Severe pain, numbness, or the swelling keeps growing.
  • It followed a head injury.
Buy at a medical shop: cold pack, elastic bandage, paracetamol.

Animal bite

Bite or scratch from a dog, cat, monkey, or another animal.

What to do now

  1. Wash the wound with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes.
  2. Apply an antiseptic such as povidone-iodine.
  3. Cover loosely. Do not stitch or tightly close it.
  4. Go to a hospital the same day.

Why the same day

  • Rabies vaccine, and sometimes immunoglobulin, must start quickly.
  • You may also need a tetanus shot and antibiotics.
  • Note the animal's type and behaviour, and whether it is vaccinated.

Do not wait

  • Every bite from an unknown or unvaccinated animal needs medical review, even if it looks small.
  • Bites on the face, hands, or neck are urgent.
Buy at a medical shop: soap, povidone-iodine, gauze. Vaccines are given at hospitals.
